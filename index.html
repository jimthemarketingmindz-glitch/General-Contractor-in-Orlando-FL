Why Hire a General Contractor?

Many homeowners start by hiring individual tradespeople one at a time. That can work for a small repair, but larger projects involve many moving parts. Someone has to decide who works first, make sure materials arrive on time, and confirm that each stage is finished properly before the next begins.

A general contractor manages that process. They typically review the plans, line up skilled trades, order materials, track the schedule, and handle communication between everyone involved. For you, that means fewer calls to chase and a clearer picture of where the project stands.

In Central Florida, there is an extra layer to consider. Summer storms, humidity, and hurricane season can affect scheduling and material handling. An experienced contractor plans for these realities instead of reacting to them.

Planning Your Project Before Work Begins

The more clearly you define the project at the start, the fewer changes you will face later. Changes during construction are one of the most common reasons budgets grow.

Define the Scope

Write down what you want to change, what must stay, and what you would like if the budget allows. Photos and sketches help the contractor understand your vision and give a more accurate estimate.

Set a Realistic Budget

Include a cushion for surprises. Older homes in particular may reveal hidden conditions once walls are opened. Ask the contractor how unexpected issues will be explained and approved before extra work begins.

Think About Timing

Ask for an estimated timeline and what could affect it, such as material lead times, inspections, and weather. A realistic schedule is more useful than an optimistic one that keeps shifting.

What to Expect During a Typical Project

Every job is different, but most well-run projects follow a similar path. A general contractor will usually guide you through:

  • An initial walkthrough and discussion of your goals and budget.
  • A written estimate describing the scope, materials, and timeline.
  • Permit applications and scheduling of inspections where required.
  • Demolition or site preparation, followed by structural and rough-in work.
  • Finish work such as flooring, paint, fixtures, and trim.
  • A final walkthrough to review the work and address any remaining items.

Ask how updates will be shared, whether by calls, messages, or site visits. Regular communication keeps small questions from becoming big problems.

Common Mistakes Homeowners Should Avoid

One common mistake is choosing a contractor on price alone. A low estimate can leave out items that appear later as extra charges. Another is starting work without a clear written scope, which makes disagreements harder to resolve.

Homeowners also sometimes pay too much up front. Ask for a payment schedule tied to project milestones, and make sure you understand it before signing. Finally, avoid frequent mid-project changes when possible. Each change can affect cost, materials, and timing.

Preparing Your Home for Construction

A little preparation makes the work smoother. Clear the work area, protect belongings you cannot move, and decide where you will cook, sleep, or work if parts of the home are unavailable. Let the contractor know about pets, children, and any access limits.

It also helps to choose finishes early. Delays in selecting fixtures, flooring, or paint colors can slow the schedule just as much as late materials.
